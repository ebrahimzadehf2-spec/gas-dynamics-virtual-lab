# Rotational MOC with fitted shocks

## Numerical model

The existing Shock–Expansion front-tracking solver is retained unchanged. The
Method of characteristics selector uses the original isentropic characteristic
solver for straight/outward-turning channels and the independent rotational
solver in `channel-moc-shocks.js` when a compression corner is present.

For constant stagnation enthalpy and a calorically perfect gas, the smooth-field
compatibility equations are

- C+, direction theta + asin(1/M): d(theta) + Q d(ln p) = 0.
- C−, direction theta − asin(1/M): d(theta) − Q d(ln p) = 0.
- Streamline, direction theta: d(ln p0) = 0.
- Q = sqrt(M² − 1)/(gamma M²).

Local pressure, stagnation pressure and constant T0 recover M, T, rho and speed.
This transports nonuniform shock-generated entropy. It does not apply one global
isentropic p0 to a rotational downstream field. The implementation uses
semi-Lagrangian characteristic feet, monotone cubic interpolation of smooth fields and iterative
coefficient/direction updates. It has numerical diffusion; increasing transverse
resolution is required to assess sensitivity. This is not an exact closed-form
field, nor a finite-volume shock-capturing scheme.

An explicit shock divides independent smooth meshes. At each new shock point,
weak-branch normal/tangential jumps and the incoming downstream characteristic
compatibility determine the shock tangent and post-shock state. Predictor/
corrector tangents advance its position. An opposing-wall hit launches a regular
reflected shock. Wall and shock boundary intersections supply characteristic
feet that do not originate on the previous station. Shock points retain both
one-sided dimensional states. T0 is constant; p0 decreases across a shock and is
transported on downstream streamlines.

References for rotational characteristics and explicit fitting:

- NACA Report 841: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19930090941
- NASA CR-3121, Appendix A, Salas, *Shock Fitting Method for Complicated
  Two-Dimensional Supersonic Flows*:
  https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19790014261/downloads/19790014261.pdf

The current implementation has a narrower topology than those general methods.

## Independent checks

`channel-moc-shocks.test.mjs` includes:

1. Four successive finite shocks for the 5-degree lower ramp / flat upper wall,
   M1 = 3: reflection positions and all shock states agree with the independent
   SciPy fixture in `wave-tracker-reference.json` to relative tolerance 2e-8.
2. A 2-degree compression / 2-degree upper expansion interaction: curved shock
   limiting states agree with an independent direct pressure/direction match
   generated by `channel-moc-shocks-reference.py`, within 1e-5 relative tolerance.
   This limiting-state comparison is before the first subsequent wall reflection;
   it is not an independent exact solution for the entire curved field.
   In addition, upstream states inside the incident centered fan converge toward
   its independent geometric characteristic relation, theta − asin(1/M) =
   atan2(y − inlet height, x).
3. Every fitted station closes normal mass/momentum, tangential velocity and
   stagnation enthalpy; entropy does not decrease. Jump residuals are below 1e-9.
4. Total enthalpy, ideal-gas consistency and nonuniform transported p0 in the
   smooth field; mirrored geometry and R scaling; one-sided profiles and
   dimensional contours; hover; every fitted station in CSV/JSON.
5. Explicit model limits for unsupported shock/shock/contact topology and
   detached/subsonic states. No isentropic field is substituted for such cases.
6. Separate retained Shock–Expansion and MOC calculations for identical inputs.

For the 2-degree shock/fan example, maximum cross-sectional mass discrepancy
falls from about 0.1605% at 31 points per smooth region to 0.0803% at 61 and
0.0401% at 121. This is an internal consistency/refinement indicator, not a
bound on every local flow error. The regular-reflection case is uniform between
fitted shocks and has mass residual near machine precision.

Run `python channel-moc-shocks-reference.py` to regenerate the independent
limiting-state fixture, and `node --test channel-moc-shocks.test.mjs` for the
new solver checks. The full application suite also retains the existing
isentropic-MOC and Shock–Expansion checks.

## Supported domain and explicit stops

The fitted topology supports one active attached shock at a time, successive
regular reflections from piecewise walls, and interaction with smooth expansion
fans. It also handles an isolated compression corner after a smooth region.
The flow and all characteristic directions must remain downstream and supersonic.

Not implemented: simultaneous shock/shock/contact topology; creating a second
shock while one is active; detached shocks or Mach reflection; subsonic regions;
automatic insertion of a new shock formed by smooth compression coalescence.
An excessive smooth compression gradient is a resolution/topology stop, not a
claim to locate every possible newly formed shock. Weak-shock compatibility
failure, mass discrepancy over 2%, a collapsed step or the 600,000-point budget
also stops marching. Only accepted upstream rows are displayed and exported.
The budget may make high-resolution long channels partial.

Two incident-shock book geometries can still be solved with the retained
Shock–Expansion method; selecting MOC reports its unsupported topology. The
application does not claim a general fully completed rotational MOC solver.
